Why your dog comes back… until there’s a duck

Your dog has a lovely recall in the lounge. In the garden, pretty good. At the park — next to the pond — it’s like you’ve vanished. That isn’t defiance. It’s maths.

Every environment comes with its own level of “interesting”. A quiet hallway is a 1 out of 10. A park full of joggers might be a 5. A duck taking off from the water a metre away? That’s an 11. When the world is more rewarding than you are, your cue gets drowned out — not because your dog doesn’t love you, but because they haven’t practised winning at that level yet.

Meet the distraction ladder

We like to picture distractions as a ladder. Each rung is a little harder than the last. Training happens one rung at a time, and you only climb when your dog is succeeding (think 8 out of 10 tries) on the rung you’re on.

Three ways to make the ducks less magnetic

1. Add distance before difficulty

If your dog can’t respond next to the pond, try from across the field. Distance turns an 11 into a 6. Get your wins there, then edge closer over many sessions.

2. Make yourself the better deal

Bring rewards that genuinely compete — the good stuff, a favourite toy, or a game of chase. When your dog does come back, celebrate like they just won a gold medal.

Distractions aren’t defiance. They’re just a rung you haven’t practised yet.

3. Never rehearse failure

A long line means your dog can’t practise ignoring you. If you call and they can’t respond, that’s information — make it easier, get three wins, then try again.
